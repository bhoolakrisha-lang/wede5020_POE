GoJu-Ryu (剛柔流)


GoJu-Ryu (剛柔流), is Japanese for "hard-soft style". In more detail, Go (剛) means "Hard",
Ju (柔) means "Soft", and Ryu (流) meaning "Style".

What is Okinawan GoJu-Ryu Karate

Gōjū-ryū () translates from Japanese to Hard Soft Style. It is one of the major traditional Okinawan styles of Karate. The name itself is a combination of hard and soft. Gō meaning hard, closed hand techniques or straight line attacks. Jū meaning soft, open hand techniques or circular movements. Gōjū-ryū uses circular and straight line attacks. The style combines hard attacks with straight line attacks like kicks and close hand punches. With soft open hand circular attacks to strike, block and control your opponent using joint locks, grappling, taking down and throwing.


Breathing is an important part in all the kata however especially within the two main kata Sanchin and Tensho. Gōjū-ryū uses a range of techniques; body conditioning, fundamental fighting principles (range, stickiness, power generation etc.) and partner exercises.

History of Okinawan Goju-Ryu Karate

The roots of Goju-Ryu Karate-Do can be traced back to the Okinawan master Kanryo Higaonna (1853-1915), who spent over a decade in Fuzhou, China to learn from Ryu Ryu Ko the Chinese martial art of White Crane Kung Fu. While in Okinawa, Higaonna transformed the techniques of Chinese martial arts into Naha-te form of karate. Higaonna's most committed and gifted pupil, Chojun Miyagi (1888-1953) travelled to China to study the origins of the art and merge linear movements with circular redirecting techniques and breathing exercises. In 1933, Miyagi sanctioned his style with the Dai Nippon Butokukai, calling it Goju-Ryu ("hard-soft style"), being the first karate system registered in Japan at that time. After World War II and Miyagi's death, it was up to important masters, like An'ichi Miyagi and Morio Higaonna to keep the system alive and promote its teachings around the world through their International Okinawan Goju-Ryu Karate-Do Federation.

Karate Senseis

About GoJu-Ryu Senseis

宮城長順 (Miyagi Chojun,
1888 - 1953)

Chojun Miyagi was born on 25 April 1888, in Okinawa. When he was five years old he became the heir to the Miyagi family. His training in karate started when he was eleven years old, under Ryuko Aragaki Sensei who practiced and taught Tomari-Te.


When he was fourteen his teacher introduced him to Higonna Kanryo Sensei.

Chojun Sensei, trained with Kanryo Sensei from 1902 until October 1916 when Kanryo Sensei died.

宮里栄 - Miyazato Eiichi
5 July 1922 till 11 December 1999

Miyazato Eiichi, was one of the leading masters of Okinawan Goju-Ryu karate.
